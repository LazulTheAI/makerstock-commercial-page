The details

Where the two products actually differ

Sales channels

BigCommerce vs multi-channel breadth

This is the single biggest structural difference. MakerStocks was built specifically for BigCommerce and Shopify merchants and syncs natively with both. Craftybase/Stocksmith covers a wider net of channels — Etsy, Amazon, WooCommerce, Wix, Weebly, Squarespace and Faire — but as of this writing does not support BigCommerce; their own help center lists it as a feature request you can vote for, not a working integration.

If BigCommerce is your storefront, that alone settles it. If you sell across five marketplaces and BigCommerce isn't one of them, Craftybase/Stocksmith's channel breadth is a genuine advantage.

Production runs

Planning and executing a batch

MakerStocks treats production run scheduling as core functionality, included from the $29/mo Starter plan: plan a run from the desktop, let an operator start and complete it from the shop floor, and stock is deducted automatically in FIFO order.

Craftybase/Stocksmith does have production scheduling, but it's gated behind their Business tier at $166–199/month — roughly 3–5× the price of MakerStocks' entry plan. Their lower tiers cover inventory, BOM and COGS, but not run scheduling.

Shop-floor access

Mobile app vs mobile browser

MakerStocks ships a dedicated mobile interface built for operators — start/complete runs, receive deliveries, check stock — without exposing pricing, financial reports or account settings.

Craftybase/Stocksmith is a responsive web app: it works from a phone's browser, but there's no purpose-built mobile experience scoped to what a floor operator actually needs to see.

Team size

Where Craftybase/Stocksmith wins outright

To be fair: Craftybase/Stocksmith includes unlimited users on every plan, including its cheapest. MakerStocks ties mobile-app seats to the plan (1 on Starter, 10 on Pro, unlimited on Business). If you have a large team and a tight budget, that's a real point in their favor.

Compliance & traceability

Batch, lot and expiry-date tracking

Both products cover this well. MakerStocks tracks lot numbers and expiry dates on every raw-material entry and deducts stock in FIFO order, prioritizing lots closest to expiry. Craftybase/Stocksmith advertises GMP-compliant batch and lot tracking with recall documentation. Neither is a compliance guarantee on its own — regulated categories like cosmetics or food still require you to follow the applicable rules — but both give you the underlying traceability data.

FAQ

Common questions

Is Craftybase the same as Stocksmith?
Yes. Craftybase rebranded to Stocksmith in 2026 — same team, same software, new name. Most existing content and reviews online are still indexed under "Craftybase," which is why you'll see both names used for the same product.
Does Craftybase (Stocksmith) integrate with BigCommerce?
Not currently. Their own help center lists BigCommerce as a feature request on a waitlist, not a supported sales channel. MakerStocks has native two-way BigCommerce sync included in every plan.
What's the cheapest way to get production run scheduling for a small workshop?
MakerStocks includes production run planning and execution starting on its $29/month Starter plan. On Craftybase/Stocksmith, that same capability only unlocks on the Business tier, priced at $166–199/month.
Does Craftybase have a mobile app for production workers?
No dedicated native app — it's a responsive website that works in a mobile browser. MakerStocks ships a purpose-built mobile interface for shop-floor operators, included from the Starter plan.
